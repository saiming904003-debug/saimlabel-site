Relief and colour

Custom 3D Embossed PVC Patches

What buyers get wrong

Two decisions, not one

A molded patch carries two independent layers of design: where the colour changes, and where the surface rises or sinks. Most artwork specifies only the first, and the second then gets decided by whoever builds the mould.

Colour zones

Each solid colour is filled separately, and the boundary between two colours is a step in the tool rather than a line of ink. That is why a sharp edge here is a moulding question, not a printing one.

Relief levels

Which area stands above the base, which sinks below it, and how many heights the design needs. This is independent of colour — a two-colour patch can still carry five levels.

Where they collide

If a colour boundary lands on a sloped relief wall, the edge reads as a smear, because the light changes across the slope. Boundaries belong on a flat plateau or a vertical wall, never on a ramp.

Artwork that survives

What disappears in the mould

Tooling cannot form a wall thinner than the mould can cut, so any element narrower than that simply is not there when the patch comes out. In practice the casualties are always the same: hairlines in a serif, the thin strokes of a script logo, small counters inside letters, dots in a registered mark, and any detail that only worked because it was printed at high resolution.

A colour gap has the same failure mode from the other direction. A thin unpainted line separating two colour zones, with no relief behind it, will close up — the two fills meet and the design loses the separation the artwork showed. The fix is a raised divider, which is a relief decision, not a colour one.

The answer is not to shrink the design. It is to decide what the patch is actually saying and drop the rest — thicken the strokes that carry meaning, replace a cluster of small marks with one raised plane, and keep the negative space wide enough that the base reads as background rather than as a groove.

Send the artwork at finished size in vector. We look at the smallest element first, mark the ones that will not form, and come back with specific edits before tooling. Retooling after a rejected sample costs more than that conversation.

Two directions, easy to confuse

Embossed, debossed, or both

Embossed

The element stands above the base surface. It catches light on its face and casts a shadow on one side, which is why lettering has to be thick enough to hold that face.

Debossed

The element is pressed below the base. It reads as a recess: shadow inside the cavity, light on the far wall. Useful for fine marks, because a cut line can be finer than a standing ridge.

Both routinely live on the same patch, and that combination is usually what makes a dense design legible: the main mark raised, the supporting linework cut in, a raised border framing both. Specify which is which on the artwork rather than leaving it to be inferred — reversing them changes the whole read.

Common failures

Where the colour and relief split goes wrong

  • Thin gap, no relief. Two colour zones separated by a hairline with nothing behind it merge into one silhouette at arm's length. Raise a divider and the separation survives.
  • Light colour on light colour. A pale zone next to a pale zone reads as flat no matter how clean the fill is, because there is no luminance difference to see. Relief is the only thing that will register.
  • Boundary on a slope. A colour change placed across a ramp smears. Move it onto a plateau or make the ramp a wall.
  • Relief deeper than the zone is wide. A colour area narrower than the step beside it gets swallowed by the moulding. Either widen the zone or lower the step.
  • Relief drawn but never specified. This is the common one: the artwork is flat, the factory guesses the depths, and the sample comes back with a relief nobody approved.
Cost

What moves the price on a molded patch

Molded PVC patches start at $0.60/pc at the 50 pcs minimum, with volume production at $0.19–$0.40/pc. Four things move the number: finished size, the number of colour zones, the number of relief levels, and the backing.

Size and colour count are the two that surprise buyers. Each solid colour zone is filled separately and gradients have to be split into layers, so a six-colour crest costs more to mould than a two-colour mark of the same size. Relief levels are the second lever and the one that goes unnoticed: a single raised plane is simpler tooling than a design that steps through three heights, and that difference is real work on the mould rather than a packaging choice.

The mould is a one-time cost tied to the silhouette and is reused on repeat orders, so a reorder of an approved design does not start from tooling again.

Backing

How the patch is fixed

Sew channel

A groove moulded into the edge for machine stitching. Permanent, lowest profile, and the usual choice when the patch is a garment label rather than a badge.

Hook-and-loop

Removable and interchangeable, with a loop panel sewn to the garment once. The right route when the patch has to come off for washing or change with the program.

Adhesive

Peel-and-stick for hard goods and packs. A temporary fix on fabric, not a production closure.

Iron-on is not a production route for this material. PVC softens under a heat press and takes the shape of whatever is pressing it, so a heat-set backing cannot be relied on to bond.

Procurement

What to send

  • Minimum — 50 pcs per design, backing included.
  • Lead time — bulk production 10–12 days after the proof is approved.
  • Artwork — AI, PDF, SVG, EPS, CDR, or a clean PNG/JPG at finished size, with the quantity and the placement.
  • Relief instruction — say which elements rise, which are recessed, and how many levels. This is the instruction that most often goes missing, and the one that decides whether the sample matches what you approved.
  • Artwork rights — band, brand, club and unit marks must be supplied by you with the right to reproduce them. We do not copy third-party trademarks or official insignia on request.
Questions

3D embossed PVC patch questions

What does embossed mean on a PVC patch?

The surface is raised in the mould above the base level. It is a separate decision from colour: a patch can be embossed with one colour or with six, and the raised areas are read from the shadow their walls cast rather than from colour contrast.

Can a patch be both embossed and colour-filled?

Yes, and that is the standard 3D molded route. Colour zones say where the colour changes; relief levels say where the surface rises or sinks. Both are cut into the same tool, so they have to be decided together.

Will thin lines survive embossing?

Often not. A stroke has to be wide enough for the mould to form a standing wall, and hairlines, fine serifs and thin script usually disappear. A narrow colour gap with no relief behind it has the same problem and can close up. Send the final-size vector and we mark the elements that will not form before tooling starts.

What is the difference between embossed and debossed?

Embossed is raised above the base surface; debossed is pressed below it. Both can sit on the same patch. Recessed lines can be cut finer than a standing ridge, so fine detail usually works better debossed while the main mark is embossed.

What is the minimum order and lead time?

50 pcs per design, from $0.60/pc at that minimum, with bulk production running 10–12 days after the proof is approved. The proof fixes colour zones, relief levels, silhouette and backing together.
