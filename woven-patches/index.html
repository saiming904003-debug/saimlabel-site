Choose it when

Choose woven patches when design clarity matters more than raised texture. They are strong for streetwear logos, team badges, complex crests and small text.

Check another route when

Avoid woven patches when the buyer expects thick raised texture. For that look, embroidery patches or chenille-style embroidery are better.

  • Iron-on backing must be tested on the buyer's fabric before bulk use.
  • Merrow borders fit regular shapes better than complex silhouettes.
  • Very fine gradients should be simplified into woven-friendly color blocks.
FAQ

Woven Patches questions, answered

Are woven patches better than embroidered patches for small text?

Usually yes. Woven patches can show finer details because the artwork is woven into the fabric rather than stitched on top.

Can woven patches be ironed on?

Yes. Heat seal backing is available. Final heat press temperature, time and pressure should be tested on the buyer's garment fabric.

Can you make custom patch shapes?

Yes. Laser cut or die cut edges are suitable for irregular shapes, while merrow borders are better for circles, rectangles and shields.

What is the best patch backing for uniforms?

Hook-and-loop is common for removable uniform patches. Sew-on is better for permanent garment attachment.
