Design logic

A back patch is read from three metres away

A back patch is not a scaled-up chest badge. At three metres the eye resolves blocks, not hairlines, so the lettering is built from solid shapes rather than thin outlines that close up at that distance. The face is woven at high density for the same reason: on a large panel the weave has more room to lose detail, not less.

Colour behaves differently too. The fire and armour in the sample above are rendered by blending six to nine thread colours across the same band, which gives the flames a graded edge that a flat four-colour print cannot hold. On a dark ground the black is woven dense rather than dyed, and that is what stops a red rim from bleeding into the type next to it.

Layout

Three pieces, not one

A club back is usually a top rocker, the centre artwork and a bottom rocker. Weaving the three as separate pieces is what lets a chapter name change later without touching the centre artwork — the rocker is its own loom file, so a reorder replaces one band instead of the whole back.

A single panel is the cheaper option when the design will not change, and it is also the right choice for artwork that runs across the whole back, where a rocker seam would cut through the image.

Durability

The border decides how long it lasts

The face is woven to hold its shape; the border is what fails first on a patch that is worn hard. A double merrow wraps the raw edge twice in a heavier thread, so the edge stays tight against leather and does not curl or fray through a season of riding, gigging or washing. A single-stitch edge is fine on a promotional patch that lives in a drawer, and the wrong choice on one that is sewn to a vest.

Price

Size sets the price, not the label list

A back patch carries far more woven area than a neck label, so the label price list does not apply. Reference tiers on this product: $0.70/pc at 100–999 pcs, $0.53/pc at 1,000–9,999, and $0.30/pc from 10,000 pcs. A pilot run at the 50 pcs minimum is quoted individually against your finished size.

Three things move the number: finished size, thread colour count, and whether the layout is one panel or three pieces. Artwork complexity alone rarely changes it — a dense dragon runs on the same weaving setup as a simple wordmark.

Procurement

What to send

  • Artwork — AI, PDF, SVG, EPS, CDR, or a clean PNG/JPG; a photo or a sketch is enough to redraw from.
  • Finished size — the flat-edge measurement, plus the garment it goes on.
  • Layout — single panel, or three pieces with the rocker text.
  • Quantity — one tier quantity per design is enough to price it.
  • Artwork rights — club, band and brand marks must be supplied by you with the right to reproduce them. We do not copy third-party trademarks on request.

The proof comes back as a digital mock-up on a vest so a committee can sign off the whole layout in one pass before the loom runs.

Questions

Large back patch questions

What size back patch fits a leather vest?

A 25 cm or 30 cm panel is the usual choice for a cut-off vest back, a 35 cm panel suits a denim jacket, and a 40 cm panel fills a full back. The three-piece layout is sized so the top rocker, centre artwork and bottom rocker sit proportionally on the same garment.

What is the minimum order for a back patch?

50 pcs per design. A chapter order that mixes back patches with chest badges runs on one invoice, one shipment and one carton list.

How long does a large woven back patch take?

Bulk production runs 10–12 days after the proof is approved. Artwork is redrawn from your file, photo or sketch first, and the digital proof on a vest mock-up comes back before the loom runs.

Can the chapter name be changed later?

Yes. In the three-piece layout the top rocker is woven as its own piece, so a chapter or location name can be swapped without redrawing the centre artwork.

Will the border curl or fray?

A double merrow border wraps the raw edge twice in a heavier thread than the face, which is what keeps the edge tight against leather and stops it curling or fraying through a season of riding and washing.

Can you match a patch we already wear?

Yes. Repeat runs use the stored loom file and the same thread dye lot, so a chapter reordering a year or two later still gets a patch that matches the one already sewn on the vest.
