Choose it when

Choose leather when the brand wants a premium, heritage or workwear signal: denim waist patches, beanie fronts, cap patches and bag labels. Debossed logos age well and read as quality.

Check another route when

Avoid leather for multi-color detailed logos. Leather shows one or two tones through emboss depth and foil; colorful artwork belongs on woven or PVC patches.

Honest buyer notes

Read before you finalize artwork

  • Genuine leather varies by batch in grain and tone; approve a physical sample when consistency matters.
  • Repeated machine washing changes genuine leather; PU is the safer route for wash-heavy garments.
  • Very fine text can close up in emboss; laser engraving holds detail better on small patches.
FAQ

Leather Patches questions, answered

Genuine leather or PU - which should I choose?

Genuine leather gives natural grain and premium aging with slight batch variation. PU gives consistent color, easier washing and lower cost, and suits vegan-positioned brands.

How is the logo applied to leather patches?

Deboss (pressed in), emboss (raised), foil press into the deboss, or laser engraving for fine lines. One or two tones reproduce best.

How are leather patches attached to garments?

Sewing is the standard route, with pre-punched stitch holes or edge stitching prepared at production so the sewing line stays clean.

Do leather patches survive washing?

PU handles repeated washing predictably. Genuine leather ages, darkens and can stiffen with heavy washing, which some denim brands actually want; confirm the expectation before bulk.
