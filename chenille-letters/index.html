Per-letter pricing

What moves the per-letter price

Chenille letters are quoted per letter, not per patch. Three variables decide the number, and all three are set by your artwork rather than by us.

Finished height

The taller the letter, the more loop yarn and the more rows of stitching it takes. A jacket-back letter and a cap-front letter are different price points for the same shape.

Loop-colour count

Each loop colour is a separate yarn run. One colour is the base case; every extra colour adds a setup and slows the loom down across the whole letter set.

Per-letter quantity

The catalog runs from $0.21/pc. A 500-piece letter set typically prices lower per piece than a 100-piece run, and 1,000-piece programs step down again.

Letter styles

Block, collegiate or script

Letters are woven in loop pile on a felt base, so the felt acts as the outline colour around the yarn. Send the letter, the height and the colours you want.

Script lettering is possible but is priced as its own design rather than as a standard letter, because the connecting strokes are drawn as one continuous shape rather than as individual characters.

FAQ

Chenille letter questions, answered

What is the minimum order for chenille letters?

50 pcs per design. A letter set is quoted letter by letter, so send the letters with their individual quantities and the whole set is priced together.

How are chenille letters priced?

Per letter, by finished height, loop-colour count and per-letter quantity. The catalog runs from $0.21/pc; a 500-piece letter set typically prices lower per piece than a 100-piece run, and 1,000-piece programs step down again.

How long does a letter order take?

Production is 10–12 days after proof approval. The proof is issued before production starts.

Can a chenille letter have a gradient?

Not as a smooth fade. Loop colours are yarn-based, so a gradient has to be split into solid colour zones. Send the artwork and we will tell you how many zones it needs.

What backing can I choose?

Sew-on, iron-on, adhesive or hook-and-loop. Iron-on needs a heat test on the final fabric before a full run.
