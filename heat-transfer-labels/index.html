Choose it when

Choose heat transfer labels when the garment touches skin directly and a sewn label would itch: activewear, baselayers, underwear and kids basics. They also carry size and care data in the same print.

Check another route when

Avoid heat transfers on high-pile fleece and heavily textured fabrics where the print cannot bond evenly. Test the transfer on the final fabric before bulk application.

Honest buyer notes

Read before you finalize artwork

  • Application settings decide durability: wrong temperature or pressure causes peeling, so run a press test on the final fabric.
  • Dark fabrics need an opacity layer planned in the artwork; confirm on the proof.
  • High-pile or waterproof-coated fabrics may not bond evenly; sample first.
FAQ

Heat Transfer Labels questions, answered

What are tagless labels?

A printed transfer applied to the garment by heat press instead of a sewn label: no edge, no itch, and no label for the customer to cut out.

Do heat transfer labels survive washing?

Yes, when applied at the supplied time, temperature and pressure settings. A wash test on the final garment fabric is part of the normal approval route.

Can one transfer include size and care information?

Yes. Brand mark, size and short care data are usually combined in one neck transfer; full care data can pair with a printed care label.

Do you apply the transfers to garments?

The transfers are supplied as sheets or rolls with application settings; the garment factory applies them on their press line.
