Woven vs Embroidered Patches: Which Should You Order?

By the SAIM Label factory team · Updated 2026-07-02 · 6 min read

Short answer: choose woven patches when your design has small text, thin lines or gradients — the artwork is woven into the fabric, so detail stays sharp. Choose embroidered patches when you want raised, tactile texture and a classic badge feel. Price is usually similar; the real decision is detail versus texture.

The construction difference

A woven patch is made like fabric: colored polyester yarns interlace on a computer loom, and your artwork is the weave. An embroidered patch starts with a base fabric (twill, usually) and a machine stitches thread on top of it. Everything else — edges, backings, shapes — follows from that one difference.

Because woven yarn sits flat, a woven patch can hold letters just a few millimeters tall. Because embroidery thread has physical thickness, small type closes up — but that same thickness is what gives embroidery its raised, premium hand feel.

When woven wins

  • Logos with small text — brand names, URLs, slogans under the mark.
  • Complex crests — multi-element artwork that embroidery would blur.
  • Hat front panels where fine lettering must stay clean at 6–8 cm wide.
  • Lightweight garments — the patch should not deform thin fabric.

When embroidery wins

  • Bold, simple logos — letters, mascots, shields with strong outlines.
  • Uniforms and clubs — the raised badge look reads as official.
  • Caps needing 3D puff — foam-backed embroidery pops on curved panels.
  • Varsity programs — chenille-style lettering for jackets and hoodies.

How cost actually behaves

Buyers often assume embroidery is the premium-priced option. In practice, at typical B2B quantities the two overlap. Embroidery pricing follows stitch count: a dense, full-coverage design costs meaningfully more than a light one at the same size. Woven pricing follows size and quantity, and barely cares how complex the artwork is. That is why detailed artwork is usually cheaper woven, while simple bold artwork can be cheaper embroidered.

Two practical notes from production: iron-on backing should always be heat-tested on your actual garment fabric before bulk, and merrow borders prefer regular shapes — irregular silhouettes get laser-cut edges instead.

FAQ

Which is cheaper, woven or embroidered patches?

At typical B2B quantities the prices are close. Embroidery cost rises with stitch count and coverage; woven cost is driven more by size and quantity. Complex, detailed artwork is usually cheaper as a woven patch.

Which lasts longer on garments?

Both survive normal garment life when sewn on. For high-abrasion positions, woven patches keep detail longer; embroidery hides surface wear better because of its texture.

Can I combine woven and embroidered elements?

Yes. A common route is an embroidered badge with a woven center panel for small text, or matching woven labels with embroidered patches in one order.

What should I send to compare both options?
