Artwork Guide for Labels and Patches: Files, Text, Color

By the SAIM Label factory team · Updated 2026-11-20 · 7 min read

Short answer: Send vector artwork (AI, PDF, SVG, EPS or CDR) with fonts converted to outlines; clear PNG or JPG works for review and redraw. Keep text sized to the construction — woven holds the smallest text, embroidery needs bolder letters, chenille takes shapes only — and expect gradients to be split into solid color zones on the proof.

Files that speed up your quote

  • Best: vector — AI, PDF, SVG, EPS or CDR, with text converted to outlines/curves.
  • Workable: high-resolution PNG or JPG on a clean background; we redraw it for production and you approve the redraw.
  • Slowest: a photo of a printed garment or a screenshot from a lookbook — still usable, but expect an extra proof round.

Send the artwork as its own file, not embedded in a tech pack page; and if brand colors matter, include Pantone references rather than screen colors.

What each construction actually needs from the file

Every trim here starts from the same artwork, but each process consumes a different production file — which is why one vector can be perfect for a woven label and unworkable as embroidery.

ConstructionProduction fileWhat that means for the artwork you send
Woven labelJacquard weave fileThe design is rebuilt in weave logic: letters and lines become warp and weft yarns. Hairlines and very small type are the first things to go.
Woven patchJacquard weave file with borderSame as a woven label, plus the border and backing become part of the design.
EmbroideryDigitized stitch fileThe artwork is converted into stitch paths before sewing — the vector itself is not what the machine sews. Fine detail is simplified at that step.
ChenilleDigitized stitch file for yarnYarn-heavy construction: bold shapes and letters only, no fine interior detail.
PVC / rubberMolded tooling from the vectorLine weights and raised or engraved layers have to be closed shapes; gradients become separate layers.
LeatherLaser or emboss path from the vectorEngraving holds fine detail better than embossing; single-weight line art is the safest starting point.

You do not have to build any of these files. Send the vector — or a clean raster that gets redrawn — and the conversion happens before the proof, which shows the finished size, the colours actually available in that construction, and anything that will not reproduce. If you already hold a production file (a DST or EMB for embroidery, a weave file, a mold drawing), send it alongside the vector.

These are starting points, not lab values — the proof shows exactly how your artwork behaves in the chosen construction before anything is produced.

Color: how yarn differs from screens

Yarn, thread and PVC are physical materials with color libraries, not RGB sliders. Expect three things: colors are matched to the nearest yarn/thread shade (Pantone references help); gradients are split into solid zones; and the number of colors affects the construction and sometimes the price. Approve color on the proof — and for critical brand colors, on a physical sample — rather than on a phone screen.

Artboard, finished size and bleed

Set the artboard to the finished size — the visible size after folding — and state the fold separately. The factory adds the fold allowance when calculating the cut size, so artwork drawn at cut size produces a label smaller than intended. How each fold changes the artwork is covered in fold types and their cut allowance.

Bleed also behaves differently from print:

  • Woven labels and patches are woven to size on a loom rather than printed on a sheet, so there is no print bleed to add. What matters is the fold allowance and keeping critical content clear of the cut edge.
  • PVC, leather and printed hang tags are cut out of a larger sheet, so leave a small safety margin beyond the finished edge for the cut to land inside.
  • Nothing needs to arrive in CMYK. Woven and embroidered colour is matched to yarn and thread shades, which is why Pantone references travel better than screen values.

What happens after you hit send

The sequence at SAIM Label: we review the artwork against the chosen construction, flag anything that will not reproduce (too-small text, gradient zones, eyelet clashes on keychains), and send a production proof showing final size, colors and construction details. Nothing is woven, stitched or molded until you approve that proof — which is why a rough first file is fine to start the conversation.

FAQ

I only have a JPG logo - can I still order?

Yes. A clear JPG or PNG is enough to start: we redraw it as vector for production and you approve the redraw before anything runs.

How many colors can a woven label have?

It depends on the weave and design; thread colors are counted and confirmed at the quote. Send the artwork and the answer comes back with the price.

Can you reproduce gradients?

Gradients are split into solid color zones for woven, embroidered and PVC constructions. The split is shown on the proof for approval.

Do I need to buy a font license?

Convert text to outlines/curves before sending and the font file itself is not needed. If you cannot, name the font and we redraw.

Do you need a DST or EMB file for embroidery?

No. Send the vector, or a clean raster we can redraw: the artwork is digitized into a stitch file before sewing, and the proof shows how the stitches will read. If you already hold a DST or EMB, send it alongside the vector so nothing has to be reinterpreted.

What size should the artboard be?

The finished size after folding, with the fold type stated separately. The factory adds the fold allowance to get the cut size, so an artboard drawn at cut size comes out smaller than intended.
