Care Label Requirements for the US and EU: A Supplier's Checklist

By the SAIM Label factory team · Updated 2026-07-02 · 7 min read

Short answer: in the US, most apparel must carry care instructions (FTC Care Labeling Rule, 16 CFR Part 423), fiber content, country of origin and the maker's identity or RN number. In the EU, fiber composition wording is mandatory under Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011, in the language of the member state where the garment is sold; care symbols are voluntary but expected, following ISO 3758. The label maker prints it — the brand or importer owns the content.

United States requirements

Three federal rules shape a US garment label:

  • Care Labeling Rule (16 CFR Part 423) — most textile wearing apparel needs a permanent, legible care label with washing or drycleaning instructions that stay readable for the garment's useful life.
  • Textile Fiber Products Identification Act (16 CFR Part 303) — generic fiber names and percentages by weight, e.g. "80% Cotton 20% Polyester".
  • Country of origin & identity — "Made in …" plus the manufacturer, importer or dealer name, or their registered RN number.

European Union requirements

  • Regulation (EU) No 1007/2011 — fiber composition must be labelled with the regulation's fiber names, durable, legible and in the official language(s) of the member state of sale. Multi-country collections usually go multi-language.
  • Care symbols are voluntary — there is no EU-wide legal care-symbol requirement, but retailers and buyers expect them. The common set follows ISO 3758, and the familiar five-symbol system is managed by GINETEX under trademark license.
  • Origin marking — not harmonized EU-wide for textiles; some member states and buyers have their own expectations. Confirm with the importer.

Pre-production checklist

Before a care label goes to print at the factory, walk these five points:

  • 1. Fiber content confirmed — from the fabric mill's spec, not from memory; percentages add to 100.
  • 2. Care process verified — symbols and wording match what the garment actually survives (wash tests, not guesses).
  • 3. Languages match the markets — one panel per language, or a multi-panel label.
  • 4. Identity & origin present — RN or company name for the US; importer details as the EU market expects.
  • 5. Size and readability — the smallest type must stay legible after cutting, folding and one wash cycle.

FAQ

Are care symbols legally required in the US?

The FTC Care Labeling Rule requires care instructions for most textile wearing apparel — as words, or as symbols the consumer can understand. Many US brands use the ASTM symbol set with short wording.

Are care symbols mandatory in the EU?

No. EU Regulation 1007/2011 makes fiber composition labelling mandatory, but care symbols are voluntary — though retailers expect them. The common symbols follow ISO 3758 and the GINETEX system, which is trademark-licensed.

Who is responsible for the label content?

The brand or importer placing the product on the market. SAIM Label produces the physical label to your approved artwork; fiber content, symbols and origin claims must be confirmed on the buyer side.

Can one label cover both US and EU markets?

Often yes — a multi-language label with fiber composition, care symbols plus wording, origin and the required company identity can be laid out as a multi-panel label. Confirm the final text with your compliance advisor.
